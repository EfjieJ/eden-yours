The Big Yes and the Wheel

The song

“The Big Yes and the Wheel” · 5 min 22

Once upon a time, even before the first hand, there was a big yes that decided.

He created biological life.

The plants grew.

The animals walked, swam, flew.

Then the man appeared - a body among the others, but with a hand that could choose.

Matter is nothing but a potential. A potential for something to become.

She waits. She's not acting alone.

It is the hand that touches it, that directs it, that gives it a shape.

The hand is the symbol of life.

It is she who touches, who takes, who poses, who creates.

Without it, the yes remains invisible. With it, the yes becomes visible on earth.

This hand stopped in front of a round tree trunk.

Instead of leaving it there, she turned it around.

The wheel was born.

The potential of wood became movement.

Since that day, all inventions have emerged from the same thing: a hand that decides, a look that measures, a yes that says "it will work."

The same yes that created life. The same hand that is the symbol. The same material that was just waiting to become.

The hand holding the flint cut the stone.

The one who held out the rope made the first bow.

The woman who planted the stick in the earth made the first lever.

Each time, it was the same hand that took a potential and made it become.

Then the hand took a stick and traced signs in the clay. Writing was born.

What was in the head could stay on the earth. A thought no longer died with the one who had it. Other hands could read it, take it back, continue it.

Later, other hands learned to count steps, angles, forces.

They drew perfect circles, straight lines that do not deceive.

They looked at the sky, the earth, the body. They noticed what was repeated.

Science was born: the gaze that measures, that verifies, that foresees.

They became professionals in geometry. They were able to achieve an expected result even before the hand moved.

A bridge that holds.

A wheel that does not break.

A boat that returns.

Each time, a potential became what we had decided.

When the steam came, the hand opened the valve.

When the electricity came, the hand closed the circuit.

When the machines started to turn on their own, the hand remained on the lever. She had not disappeared. She had just changed places.

Today, the hand holds a screen. She writes a line of code.

She adjusts a robot. Artificial intelligence calculates faster than anyone else. The robot lifts what no back could carry.

But the decision to turn them on, to stop them, to give them a purpose - it's still the hand. The big yes. The symbol of life. The one that makes potential become.

This big yes hasn't changed since the first plant, the first animal, the first wheel, the first written sign, the first measure.

It looks, it measures, it creates an expected result. He's an engineer of what he touches. Not because he owns all the machines. Because he understands mechanics and he remains the cause. Because his hand is always the symbol of life. Because matter is just a potential waiting for its hand.

The danger was never the machine. The danger is forgetting. Forgetting respect for others. Forget that the other also has one hand.

Forgetting to recognize what he created - life, plants, animals, inventions. Forgetting to give his presence. When we forget that, we think that the machine works alone. We think we have nothing left to decide. We're dark.

The one who stays awake does otherwise. He bears respect for the other. He looks at the other. He sees what the other person has done. He recognizes it.

And at the same time, he takes responsibility for his own creation. He assumes what his own hands have set in motion - the life he carries, the wheel of yesterday, writing, science, the robot of today. He does not erase what he has created. He assumes it. His hand remains the symbol of life. It continues to become the potential. Respect for others and responsibility for our own creation - these are the two things that prevent us from forgetting.

And because he assumes and respects, he doesn't have to die to start again. He can stay. On Earth. In his body. Frame by frame. Respecting each other. Keeping your hand on the lever. By no longer sinking into oblivion.

Life goes on. The wheel is still turning. The signs are still there. The hand is still there - a symbol of life. The big yes never stopped deciding. Matter has never ceased to be a potential that becomes. End.

The End
