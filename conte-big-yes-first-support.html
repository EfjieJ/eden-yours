The Big Yes and the First Support

A tale by François, in 69 pictures and on video. Watch the video, or scroll and tap “Next”: each picture follows its sentence.

In the beginning, there was nothing to push. No matter, no space, no time.

Just a yes.

A pure yes, without mass, without motion, which decided: “Let something be.”

This yes set down the first support.

Matter appeared — not as an object, but as a potential. A potential for something to become. It waited.

It did not act. It was nothing until the yes touched it.

The yes set down a second support. Energy came in.

It was not matter, but the force that could make it move.

The yes set down a third support.

Time appeared — not as a clock, but as the program that would repeat.

A sequence. A before, a during, an after.

The triangle was there: force, support, result.

But the yes stayed above. It was not in the triangle. It held it.

Biological life was born. A first cell.

Its membrane was the support — the barrier that separated the inside from the outside.

Inside, molecules pushed one another: DNA, the program; RNA, the messenger; proteins, the workers.

Each reaction was a microscopic lever.

Photosynthesis came: light pushed the electron, the electron pushed the carbon, the carbon became sugar.

The plant created nothing. It captured. It kept things flowing.

The yes had set down the supports. Life carried them out.

Muscle appeared. The filaments slid over one another, like two oars locking into stroke.

Each slide was a lever. Millions together, and an arm bent.

ATP stored energy like a spring, then released it.

The heart beat: valves as one-way levers, muscle as force, rib cage as support.

Seventy times a minute, without anyone having to think about it. An ancient program still running.

The brain took shape. Each synapse was a small electric lever.

A neurotransmitter pushed, the receptor received, the signal passed or stopped.

Enough levers close together, and something new appeared: thought. Not because a lever thought. Because the density of supports created something continuous where there had only been segments.

Then man arrived, with a hand.

The hand was the symbol of life.

It touched, it took, it placed.

It was the visible support of the invisible yes.

The man stopped in front of a tree trunk.

He made it turn.

The wheel was born.

The potential of the wood became motion.

The hand that held the flint shaped the stone.

The one that drew the string made the bow.

The one that planted the stick made the lever.

Each time, the same triangle: force, support, result. And each time, the yes that had set down the first support stayed above.

Writing came. The signs remained upon the earth.

Printing multiplied.

Calculation and science measured.

Steam pushed the piston.

Electricity closed the circuit.

Printed circuits carried information.

Chips calculated.

Satellites carried.

The internet connected.

Data centers kept.

Rockets came back and left again.

Routes through space were traced.

New worlds were settled.

Algorithms repeated.

Artificial intelligence adjusted its levers on its own.

Robots assembled.

At every stage, it was the same thing. A potential. A support. A force. A result. A program that repeated.

And above, always, the big yes that had decided it would be so.

Mechanics explained the how. The yes explained the why. Without the first postulate, without the agreement, there was nothing to measure, nothing to push, nothing to program.

The man who understood this did not lose himself in the machines. He saw the lever in the hoe, in the muscle, in the circuit, in the robot.

He saw that everything came from the same triangle. And he saw that the triangle did not turn by itself.

Someone had set down the first support. Someone had said yes.

That someone was him. It was her.

It was every point of view that remained cause. That would not sink into forgetting.

That would respect the other. That would take responsibility for what it had created.

That, one day, would no longer need to take another’s life to go on — because the source everything came from had never needed anyone to take.

Matter remained a potential. Energy remained the force. Time remained the program. The hand remained the symbol. The big yes remained above.

And creation went on. Frame by frame. Because the yes had never stopped deciding.

The End.
