The Wheel and the Gaze

A tale by François, in 42 pictures and on video. Watch the video, or scroll and tap “Next”: each picture follows its sentence.

The song

“The Gaze Is Creation” · 5 min 19

Once upon a time, there was a man who watched a stone roll down a slope.

He pushed her with his fingertip, and she went on by herself.

He understood that the movement did not come from stone - it came from gesture.

The stone was only the ground.

He cut a trunk into slices, stacked the circles on an axle, and pushed.

The charge advanced.

It was called the wheel.

But he knew it wasn't the wheel that was rolling.

He was the one who said yes.

Centuries passed.

From the wheel was born the chariot, the mill, the clock.

From the clock, the measure. Calculus, machines.

The fire, the electricity, the motor - always the same geometry, always the same signature: one hand, one gesture, one yes.

Then the machines thought.

Artificial intelligence calculated faster than any brain.

The robots walked, built, decided.

It was thought that the hand had let go.

But behind every algorithm, there was still a being who had postulated, perceived, considered.

The machine executes. The created being.

And all the while, there was something else that was moving more silently than all the inventions.

Respect.

Recognize what the other person had created.

Don't erase his gesture under his.

Because a world where everyone takes credit for everything ends up forgetting everyone.

And a world where we forget is a world that is dark.

Then the beings learned a second law, harder than geometry: responsibility.

What we create, we hold. We don't throw it away.

We don't let it fall into silence.

The creator who abandons his creation is the first to push it towards oblivion.

The creator who remains is the one who makes it last.

One day, a woman stopped for thirty seconds in front of a tree that another had planted.

She hadn't planted it.

But she looked at him as if it were her own - because the gaze is already a form of creation.

She said, "Thank you."

And the tree remained. Not because she saved him.

Because she had stopped letting him disappear.

Since that day, civilization understood that eternity on Earth did not come from a machine, nor from a formula, nor from a body that refuses to die.

It came from two simple gestures, repeated endlessly: respecting what the other person has done, and holding on to what one has done oneself.

Respect prevents the forgetting of the other. Responsibility prevents self-forgetfulness.

Together, they form the only bridge that never collapses.

We are not eternal because we live long. We are eternal because we do not stop creating, looking, and saying yes - to the other, to ourselves, to the world.

As long as there is a look that continues, nothing sinks.

The End

“Nothing sinks.”
