Monsieur Seguin’s Seventh Goat and the Light

A tale by François, in 35 pictures. Scroll or tap “Next”: the pictures follow each sentence and appear gently.

The song

“Monsieur Seguin’s Seventh Goat and the Light” · 4 min 47

Once upon a time, in a flowering valley where the grass smelled sweet, there lived a goatherd named Monsieur Seguin. He had seven goats, each one more beautiful than the last, but every one of them dreamed of freedom and ran away into the mountain.

And every time, alas, a wolf found them, and the stories ended badly.

The seventh goat, a little white kid with sparkling eyes, was different. Her name was Blanchette, and she had discovered a secret: she could feed on light.

Every morning, she climbed onto a rock, closed her eyes, and let the sun, the wind, and the invisible stars fill her with a magical warmth. This light gave her incredible energy, and she frolicked all day long without ever feeling hungry.

One day, Blanchette said to Monsieur Seguin: “Let me go to the mountain, I want to see the wildflowers and dance beneath the sky!” Monsieur Seguin, who loved her dearly, sighed: “My poor Blanchette, the wolf will eat you, like the others.” But Blanchette, with a mischievous smile, answered: “Don’t worry, I have my light.”

At dawn, Monsieur Seguin opened the stable door, and Blanchette set off, her white coat shining like a star.

In the mountain, Blanchette had more fun than ever. She leapt from rock to rock, sniffed the flowers, and sang with the birds.

At nightfall, while the moon was shining, she sensed a shadow: the wolf, with his glowing eyes and his sharp teeth.

But Blanchette did not tremble. She looked him straight in the eyes, the way you look at an old friend, and saw that he was tired, a prisoner of his hunger. “Wolf,” she said, her voice soft as a breeze, “you believe you must eat me to live, but come, I will show you a secret.”

The wolf, intrigued by this goat who glowed so brightly, came closer. Blanchette sat down on a rock, held out a daisy she had picked, and closed her eyes. A gentle light seemed to shine all around her, like an invisible campfire.

“Look, wolf, light is everywhere – in the moon, in the grass, in the air. It can feed you without your having to hunt.”

She showed him how to breathe calmly, imagining a gentle warmth entering his heart. The wolf tried, a little grumpy at first, but soon he felt a new energy, as if he no longer needed to run after his prey. “Is it true?” he whispered. “I can live without eating?”

Blanchette nodded: “Yes, wolf! The light sets you free, and we can play together under the stars!” She sprang to her feet and did a pirouette, inviting the wolf to dance. The wolf, hesitant at first, began to bound along with her, laughing like a puppy. Under the moon, they danced all night long, and the wolf forgot his hunger forever.

The next day, Blanchette and the wolf came down into the valley. They found Monsieur Seguin weeping, thinking he had lost his goat. “Monsieur Seguin!” cried Blanchette. “I’m here, and I have a new friend!”

She told how she lived on light and how she had taught the wolf to do the same. Monsieur Seguin, filled with wonder, invited them into his meadow. He too learned to find the light – in a ray of sunshine, a burst of laughter, or a flower.

Soon the whole village came to watch the goat and the wolf dance together, and everyone discovered the secret of the light.

Blanchette, the wolf, and Monsieur Seguin lived a long time, far beyond the winter, for the light kept them strong and happy. They sang, they danced, and they shared their secret with everyone, showing that no one needed to take a life in order to live. In the valley, people still told their story, the story of a goat who, with a little light, had changed a wolf’s heart and made everyone joyful forever.

The End
